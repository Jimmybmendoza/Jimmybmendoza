## I. INTRODUCTION AND RELIEF SOUGHT

Petitioner Jimmy Bladimir Mendoza respectfully requests that this Court accept jurisdiction over this special action and issue relief directing the Sedona Magistrate Court to dismiss Case No. TR2025-000093 with prejudice. This case presents two fundamental jurisdictional defects that warrant immediate appellate intervention:

**First**, the mandatory Rule 8 speedy trial deadline expired on January 26, 2026, without trial commencing and without any valid exclusion orders entered before that deadline. The State expressly acknowledged this deadline in writing on January 14, 2026, yet failed to secure a signed court order excluding time before the deadline passed. The trial court currently has a trial scheduled for February 3, 2026, which is facially beyond the Rule 8 deadline and void for lack of jurisdiction.

**Second**, all judicial officers who have issued orders in this case lack proper authorization under Arizona Supreme Court Administrative Order No. 2025-137. That order, issued on June 25, 2025, transferred assignment authority over the Sedona Municipal Court to Presiding Judge Ted Reed. Despite this clear constitutional mandate, the Sedona City Council appointed Lewis Levin and Michael Goimarac as special magistrates on July 8, 2025, thirteen days after the Supreme Court order took effect. Judge Reed never assigned these individuals to act as judicial officers in the Sedona Municipal Court. All orders they have issued are therefore void ab initio.

Both defects are fundamental, jurisdictional, and incurable through further proceedings. Special action relief is appropriate because the trial court has no adequate remedy to correct these errors, and Petitioner faces imminent irreparable harm from proceeding to trial under void judicial authority and beyond the Rule 8 deadline.

## II. JURISDICTION AND JUSTICIABILITY

This Court has jurisdiction to accept special actions pursuant to the 2025 Arizona Rules of Procedure for Special Actions, which became effective January 1, 2025. Special action relief is appropriate when: (1) there is no equally plain, speedy, and adequate remedy by appeal; (2) the issues presented are purely legal; and (3) the matter involves a question of statewide importance or presents issues requiring immediate resolution.

All three elements are satisfied here. **First**, there is no adequate remedy by appeal. If this case proceeds to trial under judges lacking proper authorization, any conviction would be void, but the Petitioner would have been subjected to an unconstitutional proceeding. Similarly, if trial proceeds beyond the Rule 8 deadline, the constitutional right to a speedy trial will have been irreparably violated regardless of the outcome. The harm cannot be remedied through post-conviction appeal.

**Second**, the issues are purely legal, requiring no factual development. The material facts are undisputed and established by the court record and official Supreme Court orders. The questions presented are whether the Rule 8 deadline expired without valid exclusions, and whether judicial officers appointed in apparent defiance of Administrative Order 2025-137 possess authority to issue orders.

**Third**, this matter involves questions of statewide importance. The proper interpretation and enforcement of Rule 8 speedy trial deadlines affects every criminal defendant in Arizona. The scope of Supreme Court administrative authority over municipal courts under Article VI, Section 3 of the Arizona Constitution is a fundamental structural question affecting the integrity of the entire judicial system. The expeditious resolution of these issues is essential to protect constitutional rights and maintain public confidence in the judiciary.

## III. STATEMENT OF FACTS

### A. Administrative Order 2025-137

On June 25, 2025, Chief Justice Ann A. Scott Timmer issued Administrative Order No. 2025-137 pursuant to Article VI, Section 3 of the Arizona Constitution. The order provides that "administrative control and oversight of day-to-day operations of the Sedona Municipal Court shall be assumed by the Honorable Ted Reed, Presiding Judge of the Superior Court of Arizona in Coconino County, and Judge Reed shall assign judicial officers as needed to conduct the court business." The order expressly states that it "shall remain in effect until a successor assumes duties as presiding judge of the Sedona Municipal Court."

No subsequent Supreme Court order has modified, rescinded, or superseded Administrative Order 2025-137, and no successor presiding judge has assumed duties. The order therefore remains in full force and effect. Under its terms, Judge Reed possesses exclusive authority to assign judicial officers to the Sedona Municipal Court.

On July 8, 2025, thirteen days after Administrative Order 2025-137 took effect, the Sedona City Council held two meetings. At a special meeting beginning at 2:30 PM, the Council conducted an executive session to review candidates Lewis Levin and Michael Goimarac for appointment as special magistrates. Two hours later, at a regular meeting beginning at 4:30 PM, the Council appointed both individuals as special magistrates to serve on an on-call basis.

These appointments were made during the effective period of Administrative Order 2025-137, which had vested assignment authority exclusively in Judge Reed. The Arizona Supreme Court Administrative Office of the Courts has confirmed in writing that it possesses no records of any assignment by Judge Reed authorizing Lewis Levin or Michael Goimarac to serve as judicial officers in the Sedona Municipal Court.

### B. Procedural History of the Criminal Case

Petitioner was arraigned on July 30, 2025. As an out-of-custody defendant charged with a misdemeanor, Rule 8.2(a)(2) of the Arizona Rules of Criminal Procedure mandates that trial commence within 180 days of arraignment. Counting forward 180 days from July 30, 2025, the last permissible day to bring Petitioner to trial was January 26, 2026.

The State is fully aware of this deadline and expressly acknowledged it in writing. In its Motion for Exclusion of Time filed January 14, 2026, the State admitted without qualification that "without the exclusion of time from the Rule 8.2 computation, trial in this matter would need to take place no later than January 26, 2026." This admission is binding and removes any factual dispute regarding the applicable deadline.

On January 22, 2026, four days before the Rule 8 deadline expired, Petitioner filed a Notice of Impending Expiration of Rule 8 Time pursuant to Rule 8.1(d). This filing placed both the trial court and the State on formal written notice that the speedy trial deadline would expire on January 26, 2026. Despite receiving this explicit warning, neither the trial court nor the State took action to preserve the prosecution within the Rule 8 period.

The State's Motion for Exclusion of Time attached a proposed order for the court to sign. However, the court never signed that proposed order before January 26, 2026. No order excluding time was entered before the Rule 8 deadline expired. As of January 27, 2026, Petitioner filed a Motion to Dismiss with Prejudice for Violation of Rule 8, which remains pending.

Trial did not commence by January 26, 2026. The trial court currently has a trial scheduled for February 3, 2026, which is eight days beyond the Rule 8 deadline and facially void for lack of jurisdiction.

### C. Judicial Authority Issues

Throughout these proceedings, orders have been issued by Lewis Levin and Michael Goimarac, who were appointed by the Sedona City Council on July 8, 2025. Neither individual was ever assigned by Judge Reed as required by Administrative Order 2025-137. Both individuals therefore lack the foundational prerequisite for exercising judicial power under the governance framework established by the Supreme Court.

On December 22, 2025, Petitioner filed a Notice of Change of Judge as a Matter of Right seeking the removal of Lewis Levin pursuant to Rule 10.2 of the Arizona Rules of Criminal Procedure. On December 27, 2025, Lewis Levin issued an order denying the Notice of Change of Judge. A judicial officer may not rule on a motion seeking his own disqualification, and Lewis Levin's ruling on this matter exceeded any conceivable judicial authority and is independently void.

## IV. ARGUMENT

### A. The Rule 8 Deadline Expired Without Valid Exclusion Orders, Requiring Mandatory Dismissal

Rule 8 of the Arizona Rules of Criminal Procedure establishes mandatory time limits within which criminal prosecutions must be brought to trial. These limits are not directory or discretionary. They are mandatory, and courts lack discretion to extend the speedy trial period absent strict adherence to the requirements set forth in Rule 8.5. When those requirements are not met, dismissal is required.

The material facts governing the Rule 8 calculation are undisputed. Petitioner was arraigned on July 30, 2025. As an out-of-custody defendant charged with a misdemeanor, Rule 8.2(a)(2) mandates that trial commence within 180 days of arraignment. The calculation is straightforward: 180 days from July 30, 2025, is January 26, 2026.

The State expressly acknowledged this deadline in its January 14, 2026 Motion for Exclusion of Time, admitting that "without the exclusion of time from the Rule 8.2 computation, trial in this matter would need to take place no later than January 26, 2026." This admission is binding and removes any factual dispute.

Despite acknowledging the deadline and despite having ample opportunity to bring the matter to trial within the statutory period, the State permitted the deadline to expire. Trial did not commence by January 26, 2026. The State's motion to exclude time was never granted by signed court order before the deadline passed. A motion is merely a request and has no legal effect unless and until it is granted by the court.

Rule 8.5 requires that a court state specific reasons for continuing a trial and permits exclusion of time only upon a finding that extraordinary circumstances exist and that delay is indispensable to the interests of justice. In *State v. Lukezic*, 143 Ariz. 60, 69 (1984), the Arizona Supreme Court held that absent such findings, a continuance may not be excluded from the Rule 8 time computation.

No valid exclusion order was entered before January 26, 2026. The Rule 8 deadline therefore expired, and the trial court lost jurisdiction to proceed to trial. Once the 180-day period expires, the court's jurisdiction to proceed is extinguished. The State's pending motion to exclude time, even if substantively meritorious, cannot revive jurisdiction that has already been lost.

Rule 8.6 mandates the consequence of a Rule 8 violation. The rule provides that if the court determines that a Rule 8 time limit has been violated, "it shall on motion of the defendant, or on its own initiative, dismiss the prosecution." The language is unambiguous: dismissal is mandatory, not discretionary. The word "shall" imposes a ministerial duty on the court to dismiss when a Rule 8 violation has occurred.

The Arizona Court of Appeals affirmed this principle in *State v. Vasko*, 193 Ariz. 142, 971 P.2d 206 (App. 1998), holding that once Rule 8 time expires without valid exclusions, dismissal is mandatory and the court lacks discretion to deny dismissal. The policy underlying this strict construction is to ensure that the constitutional and statutory right to a speedy trial is not eroded by judicial flexibility or prosecutorial delay.

### B. All Judicial Officers in This Case Lack Proper Authorization Under Administrative Order 2025-137

The second fundamental jurisdictional defect in this case is that all judicial officers who have issued orders lack proper authorization under Arizona Supreme Court Administrative Order No. 2025-137. This constitutional infirmity renders every judicial act in this case a nullity.

In *Winter v. Coor*, 144 Ariz. 56, 695 P.2d 1094 (1985), the Arizona Supreme Court definitively established that municipal courts, including magistrate courts such as the Sedona Municipal Court, are part of the integrated judicial department of this state and are subject to the administrative authority of the Supreme Court pursuant to Article VI, Section 3 of the Arizona Constitution.

Article VI, Section 3 vests the Supreme Court with administrative supervision over all courts of the state. This constitutional authority is not merely advisory. When the Supreme Court issues an administrative order pursuant to this constitutional provision, that order has the force of law and displaces contrary municipal authority for the duration of the order.

Administrative Order 2025-137 unambiguously provides that "administrative control and oversight of day-to-day operations of the Sedona Municipal Court shall be assumed by the Honorable Ted Reed, Presiding Judge of the Superior Court of Arizona in Coconino County, and Judge Reed shall assign judicial officers as needed to conduct the court business." The language is mandatory. Judge Reed "shall assign" judicial officers. The City Council's statutory authority to appoint magistrates was superseded for the duration of the Supreme Court's order.

Despite this clear constitutional mandate, the Sedona City Council appointed Lewis Levin and Michael Goimarac on July 8, 2025, thirteen days after Administrative Order 2025-137 took effect. These appointments were made by an entity that no longer possessed appointment authority during the effective period of the Supreme Court's order.

The Arizona Supreme Court Administrative Office of the Courts has confirmed in writing that it possesses no records of any assignment by Judge Reed authorizing Lewis Levin or Michael Goimarac to serve as judicial officers in the Sedona Municipal Court. Both individuals therefore lack the foundational prerequisite for exercising judicial power under the governance framework established by Administrative Order 2025-137.

An act performed by a person without authority to perform it is a legal nullity. It creates no rights, imposes no obligations, and has no legal effect whatsoever. The defect is not waivable, not subject to harmless error analysis, and not curable by ratification. Judicial acts performed without jurisdiction are void regardless of the good faith of the parties or the absence of prejudice to the litigants.

Where judicial officers lack proper authorization under applicable administrative orders, every order they issue is void ab initio, and the proceedings must be vacated in their entirety. This case cannot proceed before judges who were appointed in apparent defiance of a Supreme Court order and who were never assigned by the judicial officer vested with that authority.

### C. Lewis Levin's Ruling on the Notice of Change of Judge Is Independently Void

As an independent and additional defect, the record reflects that Lewis Levin ruled on a Notice of Change of Judge that expressly sought his removal from this case. That action exceeded any conceivable judicial authority and independently renders the ruling void.

Arizona law is unequivocal that a judge may not rule on a motion or notice that challenges the judge's own authority or impartiality. Once a notice of change of judge is filed, the challenged judicial officer is immediately divested of authority to take further substantive action in the matter, other than to effectuate reassignment as required by law.

Here, the impropriety is compounded. Mr. Levin was not merely a properly assigned judge addressing a procedural issue; he was an individual acting without any lawful assignment under Administrative Order No. 2025-137. Despite that absence of authority, he purported to adjudicate a notice that directly sought his removal. A person who lacks assignment authority cannot confer authority upon himself by denying a notice of change of judge.
